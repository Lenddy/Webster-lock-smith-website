the core logic behind these choices:
Your logo is already a strong brand — red, black, white, and gold — so the best website palette just extends those same colors rather than introducing new ones. Red for all your call-to-action buttons keeps the eye moving to what matters (phone number, contact form). The dark charcoal/black works great for a nav bar and footer, which gives the site a professional, trustworthy feel — important for a security business. The gold from the key is perfect as an accent for icons, star ratings, or "since 1949" type badges since it adds warmth without clashing.
The light gray (#F5F5F5) as a section background is a common trick to break up a page without introducing a color that competes with your branding.











Primary action (red #C8102E) exists to answer one question — "where do I click?" Every page should have one dominant action color. Yours is red. Buttons, links, the hero section. In dark mode the hero drops to deep red #8C0C1F because bright red on a dark page is too harsh.

Background (black #1A1A1A) bookends the page — navbar at top, footer at bottom. This creates a visual "frame" around your content. It also makes your logo pop since it already has a white character on dark background.

Accents (gold #D4A843) are never a background — they're highlights. Card borders, your logo text in the nav, phone numbers, copyright text in the footer, badge text, star ratings. Gold says "important but not urgent." Red says "urgent, click me."
Page bg (white/light gray) is where your content breathes. Alternating #FFFFFF and #F5F5F5 between sections creates separation without using borders.

Hover state (deep red #8C0C1F) only appears when a user's mouse is on a button. You can't see it in a static mockup — try hovering the "Call Now" button to see it darken. This tells users the button is clickable.
